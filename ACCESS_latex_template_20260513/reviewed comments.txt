# 1. Novelty — **6.5/10**

The paper itself correctly states that the contribution is an **“empirical pilot and inspectable implementation, rather than a new backbone or a validated physical instrument.”** It identifies four contributions involving checkpoint-aware classification, localisation/crop studies, matched boundary learning, and an inspectable workstation. 

That is a scientifically sensible positioning.

Your strongest novelty is **not MobileNetV4, HRNet, SegFormer, YOLO, Grad-CAM, temperature scaling, or conformal prediction**. All are established.

The stronger contribution is the combination of:

**evaluation auditing + provenance + negative-result preservation + failure-aware integration + explicit separation between proxy classification, image geometry, and physical measurement.**

That is genuinely useful.

The manuscript is particularly unusual in openly showing that:

* validation-selected and fixed-final checkpoints tell different stories;
* high segmentation IoU does not necessarily improve classification;
* crop/fusion intervention can make performance worse;
* good still-image localisation does not automatically transfer to video;
* attractive image geometry should not be presented as a physical wheel-angle measurement without a real reference.

Those are valuable findings.

However, the related-work section does not establish clearly enough that this **audit-oriented combination** is new.

Recent literature is substantially broader than the manuscript currently suggests. For example, there is recent work on tyre tread-area segmentation, visual tire-wear classification under multi-condition settings, industrial tire defect inspection, and even tire/wheel-alignment estimation. ([ScienceDirect][4])

Therefore, I would not claim novelty as:

> “a novel tyre-wear detection method.”

I would claim novelty around an **auditable evaluation framework for small-cohort vision-based tyre inspection**.

That would be much more defensible.

# 3. Dataset and experimental design — **5.5/10**

This is the most serious scientific concern.

The source collection contains **418 clean photographs but only 12 distinct tyres**, and acquisition occurred within approximately **22 minutes**. 

Furthermore, the paper acknowledges that historical folds 0 and 2 have suspected identity overlap. 

This creates an important distinction:

> **418 photographs ≠ 418 independent experimental subjects.**

The true independent unit is much closer to the **tyre/session**, not the photograph.

Your manuscript understands this very well, which is excellent. But acknowledging the problem does not completely solve the problem scientifically.

### The classification experiment has no independent test set

This is the major concern.

You explicitly state that selected checkpoint statistics are based on evaluated validation data and must not be interpreted as untouched test performance. 

Therefore, values such as:

> MobileNetV4 = 0.99746 validation-selected macro-F1

should **not be treated as evidence of strong generalisation**.

You already say this, but Table 2 and the abstract still give that value considerable prominence.

For a Q1-level paper, I would strongly prefer:

**tyre-disjoint GroupKFold / leave-one-tyre-out / leave-two-tyres-out evaluation.**

Since you already have 12 identified tyres, this may not require new image collection for the classification branch.

This is the single most important experiment I would request.

# 4. Mileage labels are proxies, not wear measurements

The manuscript correctly states that mileage groups are **source-group labels rather than measured tread severity** and that there are no measured tread depths. 

That is scientifically responsible.

But it also creates a fundamental interpretation problem.

A model could potentially learn:

* tread design,
* tyre identity,
* manufacturer,
* surface markings,
* capture position,
* lighting,
* background,
* session characteristics,

rather than wear.

Your own colour/HOG baseline variations strongly hint at this possibility.

Consequently, I would describe the classification branch as:

> **mileage-proxy recognition**

throughout.

Do not use wording suggesting actual **wear classification** unless physical wear labels are collected.

The paper already generally follows this rule; maintain it rigidly.

# 5. Classification methodology — **7/10**

The methodology is documented well.

You provide the optimizer, learning rate, weight decay, warm-up schedule, epoch budget, gradient clipping, precision, image resolutions, and batch ranges. 

That is above average.

I particularly like the model-identity audit. Quarantining nine runs because a supposed ConvNeXtV2-Small checkpoint actually had a ResNet-18 signature is exactly the type of reproducibility issue most papers hide. 

That substantially strengthens the paper's **auditability contribution**.

However, there are three methodological issues.

First, because the labels are ordinal, macro-F1 alone is incomplete. I recommend reporting an ordinal metric such as **MAE in class index and/or quadratic weighted kappa** in addition to macro-F1.

Second, Table 2 averages results across folds 0, 1, and 2 even though two folds are explicitly flagged for identity overlap. A pooled mean therefore combines conditions of different validity.

Third, Table 2 is visually ordered like an architecture ranking even though the caption says that no independent-test ranking is implied.

I would either show individual fold results or move the pooled architecture table to supplementary material.

# 6. Explainability component — **6.5/10**

I appreciate that the manuscript does not overinterpret Grad-CAM.

The paper explicitly says that saliency concentration does not demonstrate causal feature use and notes that tyre and tread regions are almost coincident, which limits the TER metric. 

That is strong scientific reasoning.

However, the gate:

> randomisation sensitivity > 0.05

requires stronger justification.

Why **0.05**?

Was that value predetermined?

Would the selected method change at 0.03 or 0.10?

Because the explanation branch influences later experimentation, the threshold should either be justified, referenced, or sensitivity-tested.

Otherwise it looks project-specific.

# 7. Dense localisation — **7.5/10**

The segmentation/localisation evaluation is fairly comprehensive:

U-Net, DeepLabV3+, SegFormer, YOLO, and RT-DETR are included.

The paper correctly acknowledges that differences in pretraining, loss functions, augmentations, and backend behaviour mean this is **not an architecture-only controlled experiment**.

That acknowledgement is important.

The finding that very high tread IoU coexists with much lower two-pixel boundary F1 is scientifically useful. It demonstrates that **area overlap and boundary fidelity answer different questions**.

I regard this as one of the stronger findings of the manuscript.

One clarification is still needed:

### Explain how “Box AP” is produced for semantic segmentation models.

For example, Table 4 reports Box AP for DeepLabV3+, SegFormer and U-Net.

Readers need an explicit statement describing how predicted semantic masks are converted into boxes before AP computation.

# 8. Crop and fusion experiment — **8/10**

This is a strong part of the paper.

Rather than assuming localisation improves classification, you tested it.

The negative results are valuable:

* tyre crop: −0.01257
* tread crop: −0.01467
* tyre+tread fusion: −0.01568
* full+tyre+tread: −0.01324

The paper also correctly interprets −0.01324 as approximately **−1.324 macro-F1 percentage points**, rather than “1.324%”. That is good reporting.

This experiment has a clear scientific conclusion:

> Better localisation does not automatically make an existing full-frame classifier better.

That is publishable insight.

The limitation is also correctly stated: because the classifier was trained on full images, cropping causes distribution shift. Therefore this experiment evaluates the intervention on a **frozen full-image classifier**, not the potential performance of a classifier trained specifically on crops.

Good.

# 9. Calibration and conformal prediction — **6/10**

This section is technically interesting but weakens the manuscript's overall focus.

The image-level calibration split is not tyre-disjoint, which you explicitly acknowledge.

The fold-0 and fold-2 temperature-scaled values of:

> ECE = 0.0000
> NLL = 0.0000

will immediately attract reviewer attention.

You correctly avoid interpreting them as deployment reliability, but I would consider moving much of this calibration material to supplementary information unless uncertainty estimation is one of the principal contributions.

There is also a conceptual issue that deserves further explanation:

The main ordinal classifier uses CORAL threshold semantics, while the ensemble confidence analysis uses an argmax over averaged class probabilities.

You mention the distinction, but readers need a precise description of **how CORAL outputs were converted into valid class probabilities before averaging**.

# 10. Matched HRNet vs SegFormer boundary experiment — **6.5/10**

The numerical finding is clear:

HRNet:

> **1.312% image-width error**

SegFormer-derived points:

> **1.721%**

Relative reduction:

> **23.75%**

The paper appropriately states that this comparison consists of **432 point records from the same 24 images and only two test tyres**, rather than 432 independent samples. This is excellent methodological restraint. 

But there are two substantial problems.

### Problem 1 — only two test tyres

This is far too small for a broad conclusion.

The manuscript itself correctly says that two tyres cannot establish a class effect.

For a stronger paper, rotate tyre identities across multiple tyre-disjoint test folds.

Since the geometry collection already uses 12 tyres—8 training, 2 validation, 2 testing—you may be able to perform grouped cross-validation **without collecting new photographs**.

That would substantially increase the paper's strength.

### Problem 2 — HRNet and SegFormer do not receive equivalent supervision

HRNet receives six point targets.

SegFormer receives dense masks.

They also use different pretrained representations and parameter counts.

The paper admits this. 

Therefore the result demonstrates:

> point-learning formulation versus segmentation-derived boundary extraction under the stated pipelines

—not—

> HRNet is intrinsically superior to SegFormer.

Your present wording is mostly careful, but this distinction needs to remain absolutely consistent.

A more powerful ablation would compare the two formulations using a shared backbone.

# 11. Statistical methodology — **6.5/10**

There is something important here that I consider a **strength**, not a weakness:

You deliberately do **not manufacture p-values from 432 correlated points**.

Many manuscripts would incorrectly perform a t-test treating all coordinates as independent.

You explicitly state that repeated seeds and point records do not increase the number of independent tyres. 

Excellent.

However, the appropriate solution is not merely “no statistical testing.”

The stronger solution is **more independent experimental units**.

If you obtain results across more tyre identities, you can provide:

* per-tyre effects,
* clustered bootstrap intervals,
* grouped cross-validation distributions,
* or subject-level paired comparisons.

Currently the evidence is descriptive.

That is appropriate, but limited.

# 15. Workstation and physical alignment — **7/10**

The workstation is useful as an engineering artifact.

I particularly like the decision to withhold geometry when model outputs are crossed, missing, clipped, or otherwise unsupported.

However, the workstation material currently occupies substantial manuscript space.

Because real wheel-angle accuracy is not measured, the alignment component is currently an **implemented software pathway**, not a validated measurement contribution.

The manuscript admits this clearly. Your future-work section correctly says that real calibration, independent wheel-angle measurement, repeated mounting, and runout assessment are needed. 

A reviewer could still ask:

> Why is the alignment subsystem a major part of this article if it has no real physical validation?

You therefore have two choices:

**Keep it**, but frame it strictly as failure-aware integration architecture.

Or **reduce it**, moving implementation details and GUI screenshots to supplementary material.

For scientific focus, I prefer the second.

# 22. Scope and coherence

This manuscript currently contains at least seven research strands:

classification → explainability → segmentation → crop/fusion → calibration/conformal prediction → point localisation → workstation/alignment.

That demonstrates effort, but it also creates a Q1-paper problem:

> **What exactly is the central scientific contribution?**

At present, the manuscript sometimes reads like an excellent audit of an entire project rather than one tightly focused research study.

I recommend making the central thesis:

> **Auditable evaluation of small-cohort vision-based tyre inspection, showing how evaluation endpoint, localisation intervention, and domain shift affect apparently strong internal performance.**

Then everything else supports that thesis.

The manuscript becomes much more coherent immediately.

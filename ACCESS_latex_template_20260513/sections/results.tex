\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}
\subsection{Classification Is Sensitive to Fold and Endpoint}
Simple baselines reveal substantial fold dependence. The colour probe obtains macro-F1 values of 0.88696, 0.46405, and 0.12322 on folds 0, 1, and 2. The histogram-of-oriented-gradients support-vector machine obtains 0.79602, 0.18182, and 0.98412. These patterns motivate scrutiny of capture-specific cues, but do not identify a unique shortcut mechanism. A historical randomly initialised ResNet-18 trained for 15 epochs averages 0.82135, whereas the later matched nine-run, 60-epoch ResNet-50 control averages 0.82332 at the final endpoint. The two controls differ in backbone and budget and are not interchangeable.

The fold-specific reconstruction in Table~\ref{tab:fold_endpoints} reveals information hidden by pooled means. On fold 1, MobileNetV4 changes from validation-selected macro-F1 0.9924 to fixed-final 0.7393, while its fixed-final QWK is 0.9381 and class-index MAE is 0.1094. ResNet-50 changes from 0.8095 to 0.6159 on the same fold, with fixed-final QWK 0.9131 and MAE 0.1563. MobileNetV4 has F1 1.0000 on folds 0 and 2 at both endpoints; ResNet-50's fold-2 F1 falls to 0.8718. These are held-group results within the acquisition cohort, not external validation. The high QWK alongside lower macro-F1 also illustrates why an ordinal task should not be described by one metric.

The full 17-configuration archive is retained alphabetically in Appendix~\ref{app:archive}. Its pooled endpoints are useful for provenance, but the primary analysis preserves folds and explicitly identifies checkpoint selection. The endpoint differences establish sensitivity of the reported conclusion; they do not prove that epoch 60 is the best policy for future data.

\subsection{Explanation Diagnostics and Same-Fold Confirmation}
The original project hypothesis that normalised tread evidence predicts cross-fold stability better than accuracy is not supported: the recorded primary and reference statistics are 0.42710 and 0.90743, respectively, across ten entries. This analysis uses legacy selected-endpoint statistics and is not relabelled as a fixed-budget result. The marking/damage hypothesis is inconclusive or undefined, and the fine-grained architecture hypothesis remains untested because its planned model arms are absent. In particular, fold-1 marking removal operates on zero active marking images; its near-zero effect cannot establish marking independence.

Table~\ref{tab:confirmation} reports the confirmation stage. Random initialisation is negative for both architectures at both endpoints. Class-weighted sampling has a positive selected effect for ConvNeXtV2-Tiny but a negative selected effect for MobileNetV4, while both final effects are positive. Uniform sampling is not consistently beneficial. The supported interpretation therefore specifies architecture, endpoint, and the existing fold. The experiment neither isolates factor interactions nor establishes a population-level sampling benefit.

The threshold analysis (Table~\ref{tab:gate_sensitivity}) finds identical initial eligibility and method choices at 0.03 and 0.05: 20 passing method rows across ten models. At 0.10, 16 method rows across eight models remain; RegNetY-016 and MobileNetV4 are excluded. Since RegNetY-016 was one of the subsequent explanation-selected architectures, the downstream selection cannot be described as threshold invariant. We preserve the original experiment and report this sensitivity instead of retrospectively choosing a gate that gives the preferred shortlist.

\subsection{Region Overlap and Boundary Fidelity Differ}
All 81 localisation runs complete 60 epochs and evaluation, corresponding to 4,860 epoch records. Table~\ref{tab:localisation} restricts the principal summary to fold 1; this retains the original detailed comparison on a challenging held-group fold and does not make fold 1 an external test. Semantic models have tread IoU between approximately 0.972 and 0.976, whereas their two-pixel boundary F1 is approximately 0.358--0.374. YOLO instance-segmentation models obtain tread IoU near 0.856--0.858 and boundary F1 near 0.189--0.226.

Figure~\ref{fig:localisation} makes the metric distinction explicit. Region overlap rewards agreement across a large interior area; boundary F1 is sensitive to contour displacement under its narrow tolerance. Neither metric alone establishes that the model has recovered the information needed for proxy classification, let alone physical tread condition. The evaluation is against one existing manual reference set rather than an independently repeated annotation standard.

\subsection{Predicted Crops and Fixed Fusion Have Negative Mean Effects}
The paired frozen-classifier analysis does not show an overall gain from the predicted crops. Mean macro-F1 changes are -0.01257 for tyre-only and -0.01467 for tread-only inputs across the 81 localisation source runs. These means do not imply that every configuration/fold pair worsens. They quantify the declared intervention on a classifier trained for full-image inputs, with no crop-specific retraining.

Fixed probability fusion also has negative mean effects (Table~\ref{tab:fusion}, Fig.~\ref{fig:fusion}). Equal tyre/tread fusion gives -0.01568; equal full/tyre/tread fusion gives -0.01324. The latter is a decrease of about 1.324 macro-F1 percentage points, not a 1.324\% relative error reduction. The result argues against assuming that localisation or additional views automatically help an existing classifier. Lost context, changed scale, crop distribution shift, localisation errors, and correlated predictions are plausible explanations, but the present design does not separate their contributions. Crop-trained recognition and learned fusion would require new experiments.

\subsection{Matched Point Learning Reduces Still-Image Error}
Table~\ref{tab:geometry} reports the fixed-final comparison, independently recomputed here from the 432 paired point records. Mean horizontal error is 1.312\% of native width for HRNet and 1.721\% for matched SegFormer, corresponding to approximately 15.10 and 19.80 native pixels. The absolute difference is 0.409 percentage points of width, or 4.70 pixels. Relative to the SegFormer mean, HRNet reduces error by 23.75\%. SegFormer raw coverage is 100\%, with zero fallback substitutions, so its raw and fallback-assisted means coincide.

HRNet has lower mean error in every seed and on each of the two test tyres (Fig.~\ref{fig:geometry}). This does not mean every individual point improves. The new-tyre example has higher error for both models than the high-mileage example, but two tyres cannot establish a class effect. The result supports HRNet as a candidate point-localisation component within this matched study. It does not establish an architecture-only advantage because supervision and initial representations differ, nor does it demonstrate downstream classification improvement or physical-angle accuracy.

A more detailed paired analysis averages six points and three seeds within each image. Point learning has lower mean error on 22 of 24 images; image-level differences (mask-derived minus point error) range from -0.556 to +0.857 percentage points of width (Fig.~\ref{fig:image_effects}). Table~\ref{tab:boundary_detail} shows that improvement is not spatially uniform: it is 0.029 at the left-middle point and 0.847 at the right-upper point. Every location has a positive mean difference, but two image averages favour the mask-derived pipeline. These retrospective summaries reveal where the aggregate gain occurs; images still share test tyres and are not independent subjects for a population significance test.

\subsection{Failure Visibility in Integration}
The saved still-image and portrait-video examples in Fig.~\ref{fig:qualitative} show the intended integration behaviour. A valid still-image result can coexist with crossed point predictions, missing mask-derived boundaries, and disagreement on a video frame. The display preserves raw proposals and withholds invalid widths or connecting geometry. There are no video point labels for a quantitative transfer test, so these examples establish observable failure modes rather than their prevalence.

A local five-call warm inference check on an NVIDIA GTX 1650 measured medians of 72.60 ms for HRNet and 33.48 ms for matched SegFormer, including preprocessing, inference, extraction, and GPU synchronisation. This is a small software check, not a controlled deployment benchmark. The current artifact also exports analysed PNGs and separately processes video at ten output frames per second; this export setting is not an inference-throughput claim. Full-window interface screenshots and synthetic alignment outputs are omitted from the scientific result figures because their small text and software-only evidence do not strengthen the three research questions.

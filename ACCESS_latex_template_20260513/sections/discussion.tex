\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}
\subsection{Answers to the Research Questions}
\textit{RQ1: checkpoint policy and held-out group.} The fold-specific analysis shows that a pooled near-perfect selected score can obscure a materially weaker fixed-final result on one group allocation. Reporting F1 with class-index MAE and QWK makes the type of error more interpretable. Recorded session membership is disjoint under the supplied tyre identities, while checkpoint selection uses the evaluated validation fold. The resulting scores describe internal group-held-out performance with an explicit selection policy.

\textit{RQ2: localisation as an intervention.} Region agreement does not ensure a downstream gain for a classifier trained on full frames. The fold-specific intervention table localises the overall negative mean: fold 1 worsens, fold 0 is unchanged, and fold 2 has small improvements. The paired design identifies the allocations on which the intervention helps or degrades the existing classifier. A crop-trained classifier or learned fusion could behave differently and requires its own controlled evaluation. Boundary F1 and IoU describe complementary spatial properties, consistent with prior boundary-sensitive evaluation work.

\textit{RQ3: boundary representation.} Point learning improves the average matched error, while the image-level and location-level analyses show nonuniform benefit. The left-middle location changes little and two image averages favour mask-derived boundaries. Both models remain potentially useful: dense masks provide a search region, while point predictions provide direct coordinate proposals. The study compares complete supervised pipelines; it does not identify the backbone, loss, or pretraining source as the cause of the difference.

\subsection{Methodological Contribution and Practical Implications}
The central contribution is the link between component evaluation and downstream utility. Pairing each region intervention with the same full-image classifier separates its observed effect from changes in classifier training. Reporting both region overlap and boundary agreement reveals whether spatial quality is concentrated in the interior or at the contour. Comparing point- and mask-derived coordinates on the same allocation then tests the representation used by the geometry stage. This sequence provides complementary evidence for component selection rather than relying on a single accuracy figure.

The findings support concrete engineering decisions. Region predictions should be assessed through their effect on the intended classifier before crop-based inference is adopted. Boundary models should be compared using coordinate error and extraction coverage together. Invalid geometry should remain inspectable even when temporal smoothing is available. The gate-sensitivity result additionally shows that the explanation shortlist depends on its eligibility threshold. These conclusions are supported by the observed interventions and diagnostics; their applicability to other collections can be tested using the same protocol.

\subsection{Scope of the Evidence}
The manually collected dataset supports controlled comparisons within its acquisition scope. Repeated images, seeds, and point coordinates remain correlated. The classification folds cover the available groups but are internal, and the fixed geometry test covers two of those groups. The 432 point records provide repeated measurements within the fixed test cohort. Results are therefore reported descriptively at the seed, image, and tyre levels, without population-level significance claims.

Mileage categories are proxies, not measured tread depth or roadworthiness. Tread design, background, illumination, or capture conditions may contribute to prediction; the baseline variation motivates that concern but does not identify its cause. The available manual labels lack a blinded repeat-annotation estimate. The point-versus-mask comparison differs in supervision, pretrained representations, and capacity. These limits define the applicable conclusions rather than invalidate the observed paired results.

Video examples expose failure but have no labelled prevalence estimate. Local timing checks do not establish sustained throughput. Target-assisted alignment is software with synthetic tests; a real reference-angle experiment is required for physical accuracy. The demonstrated outcomes concern image-space evaluation and inspection behaviour.

\subsection{Experiments That Would Add Independent Evidence}
The highest-value next boundary experiment is rotating held-out tyre groups with frozen model settings and fresh training for each split, while retaining a separate development allocation. Each held-out allocation requires models trained exclusively on its corresponding training groups. A shared-backbone point-versus-mask ablation would separate representation from backbone more directly. These extensions would test generalisation across allocations and distinguish the effects of representation and model architecture.

A blinded second annotation pass on a stratified subset would estimate label repeatability. A tyre-disjoint labelled video set would permit missing-boundary, crossing, point-error, and temporal-consistency rates. Physical claims require depth or wheel-angle references with repeated mounting and runout assessment. These studies address different uncertainties; adding more architectures on the same split would not substitute for them.

\section{Conclusion}
This work contributes a task-linked evaluation approach that connects checkpoint policy, localisation interventions, and boundary representation in vision-based tyre inspection. Fold-specific ordinal diagnostics show that pooled selected scores obscure checkpoint and group dependence. Predicted crops and fixed fusion reduce the frozen classifier's mean F1 overall, with the effect concentrated on one fold. Point learning reduces matched horizontal error by 23.75\% relative to mask-derived boundaries and improves 22 of 24 image averages, with complete comparator point coverage. The gain describes the stated pipelines and fixed evaluation cohort. These results support traceable component evaluation and failure visibility; broader transfer and physical measurement remain separate validation tasks.

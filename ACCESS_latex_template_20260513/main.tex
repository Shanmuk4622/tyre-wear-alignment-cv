\markboth{Bonala \headeretal: An Auditable Pilot Study of Vision-Based Tyre Inspection}{Bonala \headeretal: An Auditable Pilot Study of Vision-Based Tyre Inspection}
\begin{abstract}
Camera-based tyre inspection requires a distinction between recognising collection labels, locating visible boundaries, and measuring physical condition. This paper presents an auditable pilot workflow covering these tasks without treating them as interchangeable. The collection contains 418 unique photographs from 12 operator-confirmed distinct tyres and three ordered mileage-proxy classes. We analyse 153 retained classification runs, explanation-based screening, controlled training interventions, and 81 manually supervised localisation runs. Classification results depend strongly on checkpoint policy: MobileNetV4 obtains mean macro-F1 values of 0.99746 at validation-selected checkpoints and 0.91310 at the fixed-final endpoint. Two historical folds retain identity-overlap concerns. Despite high region overlap, predicted crops and fixed equal-probability fusion do not improve the frozen classifier on average; combining full, tyre, and tread views changes macro-F1 by -0.01324. A separate matched experiment uses 120 annotated images, a fixed 72/24/24 split, and three training seeds. A high-resolution point model reduces mean horizontal boundary error from 1.721\% to 1.312\% of image width relative to segmentation-derived points, with complete comparator coverage. This 23.75\% relative reduction concerns only two test tyres and differing supervision. A native workstation preserves analysed frames, displays geometric evidence and rejection states, and exposes failures on unlabelled videos. The results support an inspectable research workflow, while external generalisation, physical wear estimation, and real alignment accuracy remain unvalidated.
\end{abstract}
